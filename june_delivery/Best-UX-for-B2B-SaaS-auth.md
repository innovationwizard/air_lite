1. WorkOS — purpose-built for exactly this. SSO (SAML + OIDC) out of the box, directory sync (SCIM), organization/tenant management, and a clean API. You integrate once, every enterprise client can bring their own identity provider. Vercel, Perplexity, and Cursor use it. The UX is invisible to end users — they click "Sign in with SSO," enter their company email, get redirected to their corporate IdP, done. That's the gold standard.
2. Clerk — similar to WorkOS but more opinionated. Pre-built UI components, SSO, org management. Beautiful drop-in screens. Slightly better for the "I don't want to build any auth UI" case. Slightly worse for the "I want full control over the experience" case.
3. Cognito — AWS-native. Functional. Ugly hosted UI, painful developer experience, limited SSO configuration. You can make it work, but you'll spend days fighting it for a result that WorkOS gives you in hours.
The honest recommendation: WorkOS. Auth is not a moat. It's plumbing. The best plumbing is the kind you install once and never think about again. WorkOS costs $0 until you need enterprise SSO (then it's per-connection pricing, which your enterprise clients expect to pay for). It doesn't contradict the AWS consolidation because it's a thin authentication layer — your database, compute, storage, and business logic are all still AWS. Same way SES handles email without being "a second provider."
